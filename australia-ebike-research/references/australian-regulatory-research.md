# Australian E-bike Regulatory Research Guide

This file is a research checklist, not legal advice and not a static statement of current law. E-bike requirements can change and can differ between Commonwealth product/import obligations and state/territory road-use rules. Re-check official sources on every research run.

## Research questions

For the target model and target state/territory, establish:

1. What is the continuous rated motor power?
2. What is the peak motor power, if disclosed?
3. Is assistance pedal-dependent, throttle-operated, or both?
4. At what speed does pedal assistance cut off?
5. Does the product claim conformity to EN 15194 or another relevant standard?
6. Is there a declaration, certificate, test report or conformity marking that supports the claim?
7. What rules apply to importation and product supply?
8. What rules apply to riding on public roads, paths and shared spaces?
9. Are there age, helmet, equipment, speed or location restrictions?
10. Are transitional provisions or future effective dates relevant?

## Jurisdiction coverage

Check the current official sources for:
- Commonwealth of Australia: product safety, import and relevant national requirements.
- New South Wales.
- Victoria.
- Queensland.
- Western Australia.
- South Australia.
- Tasmania.
- Australian Capital Territory.

Do not assume that one state's road-use rules apply nationally.

## Source priority

Use:
1. Official legislation and legislative instruments.
2. State/territory government transport or road-safety agencies.
3. Commonwealth product-safety and import authorities.
4. Official standards bodies for standards status and scope.
5. Secondary legal or industry commentary only to locate issues, then verify against official sources.

Record exact source URL, jurisdiction, access date, publication/update date and effective date.

## Important interpretation safeguards

- Do not equate US Class 1/2/3 labels with Australian legal categories.
- Do not equate peak watts with continuous rated power.
- Do not assume software speed limiting alone makes a product compliant.
- Do not infer compliance from a retailer listing or marketing statement.
- Do not state a product is road legal unless the exact Australian variant and applicable jurisdiction are supported by current authoritative evidence.
- Separate product certification, import/product safety obligations and permission to ride on public infrastructure.
- Where an exact standard text is not publicly available, do not reproduce it; report the official standard reference and seek qualified confirmation.
